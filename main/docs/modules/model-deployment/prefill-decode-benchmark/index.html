Prefill and Decode Benchmark

On one H100 SXM5 node, prefill/decode (PD) isolated token generation from long-prompt processing. At concurrency 16, inter-token latency (ITL) p99 fell by 25.6×–27.8× for the two long-prompt shapes. The cost was higher time to first token (TTFT) through one Prefill replica and TCP KV transfer.

The initial suites and conclusions describe cross-Pod TCP. The separate InfiniBand RDMA comparison records the matched follow-up.

Summary

Both configurations used six serving GPUs. Aggregated serving ran three Server replicas with tensor parallelism (TP) = 2. PD ran one Prefill replica and two Decode replicas, each with TP = 2. Both used llm-d-router and the same Qwen3-32B weights.

All 16 Suite 1 cells and both Suite 2 streams completed with zero errors in each configuration. Long-prompt ITL improved under high concurrency. The mixed-load steady stream already had low ITL with aggregated serving and showed no p99 improvement with PD.

Choose PD when smoother token generation under long-prompt concurrency outweighs first-token delay and KV transfer costs. Aggregated serving favored throughput in 15 of 16 fixed-shape cells on this TCP setup. The TCP results alone do not establish the same trade-off over remote direct memory access (RDMA).

The configurations ran sequentially on the same node. Two GPUs were outside each serving allocation.

Each serving Pod exposed only its assigned two GPUs and had a private /dev/shm and IPC namespace. hostIPC was disabled, and no RDMA interface was attached. NVLink carried the intra-Pod TP traffic. All cross-Pod communication used TCP sockets over eth0, including Mooncake KV transfer between roles.

Suite 2: Mixed load

The steady stream sent 64 requests with 512 input and 256 output tokens at concurrency 4. The concurrent burst stream sent 40 requests with 4,096–8,192 target input tokens and 64 output tokens. Its concurrency was 4, with a two-second burst interval. Both streams finished with zero errors. Each stream’s throughput uses the entire mixed-load duration, including burst waits and the final interval. It is not that stream’s saturated throughput.

Burst prompt lengths were unseeded random samples from [4,096, 8,192]. Observed mean lengths were 5,792.275 tokens for aggregated serving and 5,893.1 for PD. The streams therefore used different prompt sets.

Steady PD 64/64 217.862 1,542.731 13.635 17.518 246.150
Burst Aggregated 40/40 507.856 860.598 14.208 18.078 42.361
Burst PD 40/40 1,264.500 2,555.799 13.834 17.826 38.461

The steady stream’s ITL p99 was 16.925 ms for aggregated serving and 17.518 ms for PD. This mixed-load recording shows no steady-stream p99 reduction. The maximum steady-stream token gap fell from 486.353 ms to 20.850 ms, a 23.3× reduction.

The burst stream’s ITL p99 was 18.078 versus 17.826 ms. Its maximum fell from 486.248 to 20.663 ms, a 23.5× reduction. Maximum gaps and p99 describe different parts of the distribution.

Per-request TPOT p99 fell from 18.708 to 13.863 ms on the steady stream. On the burst stream, it fell from 21.420 to 14.171 ms. These per-request averages describe a different distribution from individual token gaps measured by ITL.

KV transfer

Mooncake producer logs contain 67 APIServer metric windows, with throughput from 82.827 to 1,516.976 MB/s. One window reports 1,390.73 MB/s for 48.0 MB transferred in 34.514 ms. If that rate applies independently to each TP rank, two ranks give approximately 2.78 GB/s. The log labels do not identify each rank, and no synchronized rank counters verify that aggregate estimate.

Every captured metric window reported zero failed transfers and zero failed receives.

Benefits

  • At 4096/128, concurrency 16, ITL p99 fell from 458.058 ms to 17.895 ms, a 25.6× reduction.
  • At 8192/128, concurrency 16, ITL p99 fell from 509.188 ms to 18.315 ms, a 27.8× reduction.
  • At 256/1024, concurrency 16, output throughput rose from 753.604 to 1,078.624 tok/s, a 43.1% gain.

Separating prompt processing protected Decode token gaps under heavy long-prompt concurrency. The decode-heavy throughput comparison includes an aggregated TTFT outlier of 30,043.341 ms. Its TTFT p50 and p95 were 113.217 and 177.494 ms; p99 equaled the maximum outlier. That single tail outlier extended the aggregated wall time to 86.913 seconds, versus 60.735 seconds for PD.

Its cause is unresolved. The observed gain needs a repeat run before attributing it to role separation.

Trade-offs

The single Prefill replica queued long prompts while TCP moved their KV tensors. At 4096/128, concurrency 16, TTFT p50 rose from 1,231.691 ms to 8,065.377 ms. At 8192/128, it rose from 2,072.452 ms to 17,514.203 ms. The isolated decode tail therefore came with substantially slower first-token delivery.

Aggregated serving had higher output throughput in 15 of 16 fixed-shape cells. At 8192/128, concurrency 16, it reached 322.852 tok/s against 98.154 tok/s for PD. In Suite 2, aggregated serving also had lower TTFT and higher throughput on both streams. Choose PD for the measured decode-isolation benefit only when that latency trade-off fits the workload.

InfiniBand RDMA comparison

A separate follow-up used 8 × H100 SXM5 and 8 × ConnectX-7 InfiniBand adapters on one GPU node. The adapter inventory was mlx5_0–mlx5_7; all roles remained on gpu-node-0. The allocation remained one Prefill and two Decode replicas, with two GPUs and TP = 2 per replica. The 16 fixed-shape cells completed 640/640 requests with zero errors.

Configuration

Update pd-deployment.yaml from the TCP walkthrough :

diff
 spec:
+  kvTransfer:
+    protocol: RDMA
   roles:
     - name: prefill
       kind: Prefill
       resources:
         accelerator: "2"
+        interface: "2"
     - name: decode
       kind: Decode
       resources:
         accelerator: "2"
+        interface: "2"

Intra-Pod TP still used NVLink; the RDMA setting applied to cross-Pod Mooncake KV transfer.

Mooncake TP workers logged protocol rdma, KV-cache registration and RDMA-ready acknowledgements. The captured Decode workers enumerated mlx5_6 and mlx5_7, with LIDs 21816 and 21819. This runtime evidence establishes the InfiniBand KV path; HTTP routing and bootstrap control traffic remain separate.

The final access capture counted 809 Prefill completions and 407 + 402 Decode completions. These include warm-up and calibration; they are broader than the 744 measured client requests.

At 4096/128, concurrency 16, RDMA reduced median TTFT 4.10× and raised throughput 2.45× against TCP PD. At 8192/128, concurrency 16, median TTFT fell 2.80×, with 2.41× higher throughput. Long-prompt transfer improved without sacrificing the separated Decode role.

At 8192/128, the values were 2,072.452 versus 6,256.258 ms. RDMA throughput remained below aggregated serving in both cells: 475.153 versus 490.425 tok/s, and 237.019 versus 322.852 tok/s.

Mixed-load comparison

The follow-up completed 64 steady and 40 burst requests with zero errors, for 744/744 measured requests across both suites. Per-stream throughput uses the full mixed window, including burst sleep intervals: 60.260 s for RDMA versus 66.561 s for TCP. Burst prompts were unseeded samples from the same target range, not a matched-prompt replay.

Stream TCP TTFT p50 (ms) RDMA TTFT p50 (ms) TCP ITL p99 (ms) RDMA ITL p99 (ms) TCP output (tok/s) RDMA output (tok/s)
Steady 217.862 140.293 17.518 17.234 246.150 271.887
Burst 1,264.500 619.482 17.826 17.987 38.461 42.482

RDMA lowered median TTFT 1.55× on the steady stream and 2.04× on the burst stream. Maximum ITL remained close to TCP: 20.895 versus 20.850 ms for steady traffic, and 21.036 versus 20.663 ms for bursts. Burst ITL p99 increased slightly from 17.826 to 17.987 ms.

Remaining trade-offs

RDMA did not improve every latency metric. At 512/256, median TTFT rose from 192.224 to 215.574 ms at concurrency 8 and from 261.322 to 372.135 ms at concurrency 16. At 256/1024, concurrency 16, it rose from 198.038 to 302.155 ms. The 256/1024, concurrency-4 output throughput also fell slightly: 291.832 to 290.996 tok/s.

The captured RDMA run used larger KV blocks than the engine default. It does not establish the same results with default RDMA block sizes. Separate runs, small prompt-length variations and early stopping prevent a transport-only causal or equal-token-work claim.

Limitations

The TCP baseline and RDMA follow-up cover one model and one pass per configuration on single-node H100 setups. They do not measure multiple GPU nodes, additional Prefill replicas or other model sizes. Intra-Pod TP uses NVLink; cross-role KV transfer uses TCP or InfiniBand according to the capture. Neither capture measures KV transfer over NVLink.

The mixed burst used a range of prompt lengths, with different observed lengths between configurations. It is not a matched-prompt replay. The fixed-shape samples also have small token-count variations. Requested output lengths are caps; some requests ended earlier. Throughput uses actual output token counts. Zero client errors establish completion for these samples, not a general availability guarantee.
