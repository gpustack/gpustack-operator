Model Deployment Scaling Simulation

A deterministic local simulation compares the three ModelDeploymentScaler algorithms on identical synthetic traffic. It records replica adjustments, request outcomes, time to first token (TTFT), time per output token (TPOT) and inter-token latency (ITL). These results are not hardware measurements and are not tuning or purchasing recommendations.

The measured hardware comparison — including a four-node H200 sensitivity wave scored against the same true-SLO and goodput metrics — lives in Model Deployment Scaling Benchmark ; the scaler contract lives in Model Deployment Scaling .

Fixture and provenance

Every configuration uses the same synthetic plant, fleet lifecycle and clock. Each receives the same open-loop arrivals for a given scenario seed; only the scaler algorithm differs. Runs replay identically for each seed. A separate fixed Shadow control observes the same traffic without committing changes.

Item Value
Engine vLLM exposition (synthetic plant counters and histograms)
Plant capacity 300 tokens/s per replica, batch 4, batch gain 0.05, contention 0.02
Request timeout 30 s
SLO targets TTFT 2 s; ITL 100 ms; no TPOT target
Fleet 1 initial replica; quota 6 (4 in quota-shortfall); 10 s admission; 20 s boot; 30 s retirement
Scaler 10 s sampling; maxReplicas 6; scale-up step 1; 60 s scale-down stabilization
Configurations Proportional on QueueDepth target 4 tolerance 0.1; TokenThroughput at 300 tokens/s; Predictive with the same capacity, 30 s horizon and 4-point warm-up; fixed Shadow control
Traffic Open loop, 1 s steps, jittered request lengths, quantized to whole tokens per request; scenario seeds 20261026 through 20261034
Token truth Opt-in whole-token recording; TTFT, TPOT and ITL statistics derive from recorded token timestamps

Scenarios: idle (no traffic), step (1 to 3 requests/s at 60 s), burst-fault-reset (burst, a 70–90 s scrape outage, a counter reset at 100 s and a controller restart at 120 s), holdout-cache-shift (prefix-cache fraction rises from 0 to 0.5 at 120 s; holdout), and noise (constant 2 requests/s with strongly jittered lengths).

The remaining scenarios are ramp (0.5 to 4 requests/s in five steps), quota-ceiling (10 requests/s against a 6-replica quota equal to the scaler maximum), quota-shortfall (8 requests/s against a 4-replica quota below the maximum, then 0.5 requests/s), and length-shift (50/50-token requests switch to 200/400-token requests at 120 s).

The fixture lives beside the scaler controller tests. Its comparison suite rewrites two deterministic artifacts in the OS temp directory: a summary for each scenario and configuration, with provenance notes, and a timeline with one sampled instant per line. The timeline records offered traffic, signals read by each algorithm (null when missing), the raw recommendation, committed count, ready replicas and reasons.

Idle rows carry null fractions because no request was offered; the absence of traffic is not a 0% or 100% compliance measurement.

Three behaviors are visible. First, Proportional overshoots its raw recommendation under a queue backlog (18 at 110 s) because one instantaneous queue depth reflects the whole backlog, while the windowed algorithms stay nearer capacity. Second, every configuration shows Missing throughput windows while newly committed replicas boot (110–130 s for Proportional); the timeline records those as missing readings, not zeros.

Third, all three configurations reach 6 replicas during 150–230 s, after which TokenThroughput and Predictive scale down to 5 at 240 s while Proportional holds 6 — the source of their differing change counts. In the ramp scenario the differences do change outcomes: Predictive committed its first growth 15 s after the load event against 55 s for the reactive configurations and completed 99.4% of requests within the true SLO against 76.1%.

Quota shortfall

quota-shortfall caps the fleet at 4 replicas while the scaler maximum stays 6, so the overload phase strands desired replicas in the pending cohort with admission pinned at the quota and the block reason ResourceExhausted. When a cohort has waited pendingTimeoutSeconds (60 s), the scaler rolls back to its last committed count of 4 while the raw recommendation still exceeds the quota — 60 for Proportional at 100 s, 6 for the token configurations at 130 s.

When demand later drops to 0.5 requests/s, the recommendation falls to 4 or fewer and no new cohort forms.

Clearance here is the pending timeout’s rollback, not a quota change. The fixture contains no quota raise, so recovery from an enlarged quota is not exercised and remains unverified.

Reading the numbers

  • True TTFT, TPOT and ITL statistics are computed from recorded whole-token timestamps. TTFT and TPOT percentiles weight completed requests; ITL percentiles pool every adjacent gap of completed requests, so longer responses contribute more samples. Failed partial traces and outstanding requests enter no percentile but stay non-compliant in the offered-based SLO denominator.
  • The timeline distinguishes missing readings from true zeros. A gauge or latency field is null unless the sampler read it fresh; the observed token rate is null while the throughput signal is missing. A zero appears only where the sampler read a fresh zero.
  • The plant’s exposition histograms and per-request latency summaries are per-step approximations of the engine metrics the configurations consume. They are reported separately as observed values and are never relabeled as the timestamp-derived statistics above. In these scenarios the two coincide because per-request service rates stay constant within a request; they diverge when the rate changes between quanta, which the plant’s own boundary tests cover.
  • No TPOT SLO target is configured. TPOT is reported as a distribution only and is never scored.
  • Proportional reads QueueDepth against its target. It does not read TTFT, ITL or TPOT; those appear in its rows only as evaluation metrics.

Limitations

  • The plant is a synthetic constant-rate service model with one capacity setting. It exercises the scaler’s control loop, not engine or accelerator behavior; no hardware conclusion follows from it.
  • The controller restart in burst-fault-reset reconstructs the scaler and sampler state; it is not a serving Pod restart.
  • noise jitters correlated request lengths, not the arrival rate; ramp is a five-step rate ramp.
  • Holdout scenarios are reported unmodified and no configuration is tuned on them within this fixture. That demonstrates independence from the current configuration only; it does not verify historical tuning.
  • quota-shortfall clears its pending cohort through the pending timeout’s rollback. No quota-raise or capacity-release event exists in the fixture.
  • The comparison records measurements and does not declare a winning algorithm. Recommendation differences under one synthetic capacity do not establish general algorithm superiority.
